*9 - Which is more probable and why: that technological developments will reduce class distinctions or that they will increase them? Consider both the short and long term. Consider the effects both within a single nation and between nations in various parts of the world.*

In the short term, history has consistently shown that the wealthy and powerful will aggregate wealth, increasing class distinctions. The monicker "late stage capitalism" has recently been used to describe this trend, especially within the West. However, in the long view, increased access to technology should increase economic equality. Throughout history we have seen repeated diffusions of technology which, while at first limited to the upper class, are eventually affordable for the layperson. 

*27 - Discuss and expand upon the theme that one measure of a civilization is the size of the set of tasks that its citizens can perform without having to think about them (i.e. they are abstracted).*

One way to major step in abstraction is the invention of more advanced transport networks. A hunter-gather needed to physically walk or run from place to place, and had to keep their body in the shape to do so. Agricultural societies had to learn husbandry, so that they could travel from place to place on the backs of animals and in carts. An industrialized society abstracts much of this away, as only a small subset of workers in factories understand completely how vehicles function. Laypeople had to know how to shift gears and do some basic maintenance, while also pressing the gas pedal. Finally, in this information age, we have further advanced transportation. Now, you can transfer some digital currency, and a driver will pull up to your location. Electric and autonomous cars make personal driving easier, with either a single pedal or no pedal needed to drive. This transition shows how as civilization matures, the individuals within it become more divorced from the transportation that they use.
