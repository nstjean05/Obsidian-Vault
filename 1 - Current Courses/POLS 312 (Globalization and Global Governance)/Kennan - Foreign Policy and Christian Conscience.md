#AISummary 
#### Intro: method over purpose
- Christian concern fits **method** better than **purpose** in foreign policy
- Hard to know which government undertakings have Christian significance
    - Cause and effect are very hard to gauge in advance
    - _Irony_ between statesmen's intentions and results (Butterfield, Niebuhr)
    - Consequences almost never match what was intended
    - Statesmen are best guided by firm, sound principle, not just foresight and calculation
- Method is different: a govt can't fully know _what_ it's doing, but it knows _how_
    - Good methods will be useful and bad ones pernicious
    - Patient, conciliatory, honest, humane vs petty, devious, self-righteous
    - Bad behaviour pollutes worthy purposes; good manners redeem even disastrous undertakings
    - So a Christian citizen is on solid ground scrutinizing diplomatic methods, even when unsure about purposes
- Many questions have no clear Christian significance
    - Conflicts of commercial interest, prestige, fear
    - Ex. Common Market vs free trade area, Iceland fishing rights, Kashmir
- Open question: do the cold war, colonialism, aid, the UN, and the atom involve Christian values?
### Our competition with Moscow
- States can create a decent atmosphere for personal moral life, or foster cruelty, suspicion, terror, and brutality
    - Ex. Hitler, Stalin, Chinese Communists
    - Christianity can't be indifferent to those doctrines; preventing their spread serves a Christian purpose
- But not everything that hurts Communism is good, and not everything a Communist govt accepts is bad
    - Competition with Moscow isn't the only reality in IR
    - Must consider the interests of peoples under Communist rule, not just their govts
    - Righteous purposes don't excuse indecent methods
    - Copying the adversary's methods means losing before the battle starts
- Communism isn't static
    - **Totalitarian state** is an abomination, but the **conservative authoritarian state** (the Western norm in the Christian era) is not
    - USSR (unlike China) has been moving toward the latter since Stalin
        - Ex. ended punishing whole categories of people for abstract or preventive reasons
- Democracy vs traditional authoritarianism: differences are _relative_, not clear-cut
    - Authoritarian regimes often have wide popular acceptance
    - Democracies have lobbies, parties, and small groups controlling nominations, which weaken representativeness
    - A democracy's Christian value depends on how its liberties are _used_
        - Raises questions about American society, though not grounds for despair
- Conclusion: our wants aren't automatically God's purpose, and the Russians' aren't the devil's
    - Pattern is "complex, fuzzy, and unstable"; judge each issue, and accept what we can't know
    - Some conflicts might draw only pity and disgust from God toward _both_ sides
### The Tragedy of Colonialism
- Common assumptions today
    - Any foreign rule is worse than indigenous rule
    - Any anti-colonial effort is automatically good, i.e. self-determination is a Christian purpose
- Kennan says there's no justification for either
- Colonialism wasn't a moral act but a response to historical conditions
    - Industrialism arrived in Europe and North America 100+ years earlier, creating huge disparities in physical and administrative power
    - US avoided it only because it was busy developing its own continent
- Its technological and psychological justification has worn out in many (not all) cases
    - Liquidation produces bitter tensions with little Christian meaning
    - Mother countries: selfishness and shortsightedness, but also real responsibility
    - Colonial peoples: real love of liberty, but often chauvinism, hatred, irresponsibility
- Christian posture: see these as _tragic situations_ where right and wrong are indistinguishable
    - US is a conspicuous failure on racial problems
    - Approach with humility, detachment, compassion for both sides
    - Don't invoke Christ's judgment on situations beyond mortal power to prevent or liquidate painlessly
#### Foreign aid
- Argues against absolutes; asks what _charity_ really is
    - Charity isn't giving what lets people avoid facing long-run necessities
    - Skeptical of the handout, even in personal life
- Harder between nations
    - Aid alters internal competition, so some benefit and others lose
    - Recipients must see it as Christian charity, but most peoples don't believe govts act selflessly
        - Without a clear self-interest, they invent a worse one
- Aid has a place, but its possibilities are slimmer than assumed
    - Fewer outright grants is better
    - Frame it as rational national interest, not disinterested altruism
### The UN As a Symbol of Conscience
- The **sovereign national state** has no foundation in Christian principle
    - Christ never taught that humanity should divide into families that each answer to no higher authority
    - A truly Christian foreign policy would need to overcome that state egotism
- The UN doesn't actually transcend sovereignty
    - Forum for govts to communicate, not a supergovernment
    - Members are govts, not peoples; its authority is conferred by sovereign states
- No special Christian sanctity attaches to majority decisions (small countries aren't more virtuous)
- But the UN is the _germ_ of a conscience higher than the national one, a "fellowship of fate"
    - A satisfactory future depends on that consciousness growing
    - It's a weak but real symbol: cherish it, don't overburden it, strengthen it
    - Not every UN decision is automatically right, and not every issue belongs there
    - If the idea disappears, nothing stands between us and a chaotic world in the atomic age
### The Moral Implications of War
- Here a Christian _can_ take a stand: issues of civilization's interest (war, the atom, mass destruction weapons)
- War has always posed unresolved dilemmas for Christian thought
- Disagrees with the Quakers' renunciation of power
    - Domestic and international affairs are intimately linked (civil wars have international implications, and vice versa)
    - Can't condemn coercion internationally but tolerate it domestically
- Still, we should care about _how_ force is applied
    - Moral distinctions among weapons are relative (ex. gunpowder was once viewed with horror), but still meaningful
    - Weapons of **indiscriminate mass destruction** exceed what Christian ethic can accept
    - Older weapons were _discriminate_
        - Linked directly to political aims (evacuate, desist, accept authority)
        - Pre-WWI distinction between armed forces and civilians
        - War law didn't allow punishing whole peoples as blackmail on their govts
    - Atomic weapons offend, as did WWII area bombing
        - Ex. Dresden, Hamburg, Hiroshima, Nagasaki: Americans went beyond what Christian conscience should allow (though the problem wasn't easy)
- Kennan regrets this as an American and a Christian
    - Defend homes directly, but don't make noncombatants hostages for their govts' behaviour
- Objection: "This means defeat"
    - He's skeptical of "victory" and "defeat" in modern great power war; the defeat _is_ war
    - Sometimes we must follow conscience and trust God's mercy

### Atomic Testing

- Scientists advise him to stay away (disagreement, unknowns, he's a layman), but public facts still raise a conscience problem
- Reports from 1958
    - Strontium fall-out on New York up 25% in the first eight months
    - Los Angeles: radioactivity 500 to 1,000 times normal for limited periods
    - Sweden: radioactivity five times higher at 10 km altitude, with unusually large particles; Brazil similar
- Much fall-out is still in the upper atmosphere, and health effects are _cumulative_
- Kennan is amazed at reassurances from scientists ("negligible", few deaths vs natural causes)
    - Ex. 100 deaths from an explosion vs 102 from Sweden's radioactivity
    - No right as Christians to take even one innocent life, let alone 102 or 102,000
    - The Sixth Commandment has no quantitative stipulation
- Welcomes the govt's readiness to end tests; wants to go further
    - Eliminate use of atomic weapons in war
    - Free ourselves from dependence on them and from the commitment to _first use_

### Our Obligation to the Future

- Principle extends beyond weapons to modern technology
    - We're **custodians, not owners**, of the earth
    - Obligations to past and future generations are as solemn as to ourselves
    - No justification for altering the environment for our own safety or convenience in ways that change life for those after us
- Moral laws assume a certain natural environment, reflecting God's purpose
    - We didn't create it and have no right to destroy it
- Unknown effects of
    - Atomic tests
    - Disposal of radioactive wastes
    - Detergents and oil fouling the sea
    - Reckless use of insecticides
- Christians must acknowledge responsibility; most of these are international
- Closing
    - Political motives are ambiguous in the Christian sense, so don't pour Christian enthusiasm into vessels built for practical politics
    - Look for Christian meaning in
        1. Methods of diplomacy (decency and humanity always serve the Christian cause)
        2. The apocalyptic dangers threatening the continuity of history
    - Man, driven from Eden by his own folly, must not commit the blasphemous act of destroying the world he's been allowed to live in
