### Chapter 5: After the Fall of Caesar
- Interregnum will end, and a new or revised world order will emerge
- Ch. 4's Caesarisms can't replace liberal internationalism by themselves
    - Ex. Putin's illiberal authoritarianism; Musk, Zuckerberg, Thiel; Al-Qaeda and ISIS; Trumpism
- This chapter asks which _assemblages of power_ could form a new **historic bloc**
- **Robert Cox**: an assemblage of power has three parts
    1. Social relations of production
    2. Form of state
    3. World order (product of the first two, but pushes back on them)
    - Built from three basic elements: material capabilities, ideologies, institutions
- Digital revolution, ideologies of decentralization and horizontalism, and network institutions change all three levels
- Passing liberal order: US protected and promoted an industrial global economy
    - Liberal states, free trade, democracy promotion, resisting communism
    - End of Cold War and globalization were the high-water mark; digitalization then undermined its legitimacy
- Four possible assemblages (speculation based on trends, _not_ predictions)
    1. **Empire**
    2. **Illiberal international order** with China as hegemon
    3. **Neomedievalism** (new "Dark Age")
    4. **Postcapitalism**

### Empire

- Hardt and Negri (2000): power shifts from sovereign states to a decentered global sovereign network
    - Declining state sovereignty is a primary symptom of coming Empire
    - "Decentered and deterritorializing apparatus of rule" with open, expanding frontiers
    - Unlike old imperialism, _no single hegemon_
    - Global governance as a sovereign layer: manages the integrated market, spreads human rights norms and (mostly Western) civilization standards, builds international legal codes
- Project was never completed, so it works as a model for a post-Caesarist order

#### Social relations of digital production

- Premium on _nonmaterial_ factors of production
    - Robotics, 3D printing, digital comms make old industrial methods supplement information production
- Decentralized network: each step is an autonomous node in a "rhizome"
    - Ex. smartphone: cheap manufacturing in China/India, valuable proprietary software designed by well-paid Western engineers
    - Few barriers or regulatory points; every person on Earth is a worker or customer
- **Immaterial labour** (Hardt and Negri): research and design, advertising, service, care and affective work
    - Some lucrative and skilled, but most is contingent, exhausting, sometimes unpaid (ex. child rearing)
- **Precariat** (Guy Standing): short-term contracts, underpayment, demands for flexibility
    - A _class-in-the-making_ that could become a counterhegemonic force

#### Sovereign actors in Empire

- Nation-state no longer the right unit to make and enforce law
- Pyramid-like **constitutional sovereignty**
    - Top: US (central) plus G7, UN, NATO, IMF as managing partners and enforcers
    - Base: transnational corporations, NGOs, elite intellectual networks
    - Informal spaces where deals are made before formalization
        - Ex. CORE (NY), Capital Club (Dubai), World Economic Forum at Davos
- Ex. Greece: the **troika** (IMF, European Commission, ECB) backed by France and Germany forced austerity for bailout money
    - Varoufakis: leaders had invested too much political capital to back down, so Greece had to keep suffering
- Ex. Russia-Ukraine: resistance is an assemblage of Western states, NATO, and power-broker conferences, not just Ukraine's military

#### World order in Empire

- Unipolar US hegemony gives way to a dispersed authority apparatus, so balance of power among states loses meaning
- Sovereign claim extends over every territorial and virtual space (not seen since Rome)
    - Less distinction between inside and outside
- Conflicts are _civil conflicts_ between factions inside the apparatus, not clashes of blocs
    - War takes hybrid forms; goal is stability of the order, not defeating an adversary
- **Multitude**: counterforce created by new circuits of cooperation across nations
    - Formed by digital revolution and global communications
    - Paradox: in an age of communication, struggles are "incommunicable" (information overload blocks solidarity)
    - Until solved, Empire stays prevailing and can manage uprisings easily

### Rise of the Middle Kingdom

- China now stands alongside the US; many see a new bipolar order, even a path to world war
    - GDP from $5.7T (2004) to ~$35T (2024 est.), $10T more than the US by this measure
    - Lifted 500M+ people out of extreme poverty
    - RAND report: parity or advantage in six of nine conventional areas in the South China Sea
- **Beijing Consensus**: autonomous development model, largely free of Western supervision
    - Own agencies, institutions, guidelines
- Still sits inside a crisis-ridden liberal internationalism and remains vulnerable to it
- Question: can China be the hub of a new order?

#### Social and economic relations of a Chinese historic bloc

- Mao's reworking of Marx: Confucian approach to class and state-society relations, peasants as agents
- Deng Xiaoping reforms (1977): eased state supervision, modest liberal reforms, exposure to the liberal world economy
    - **Town and village enterprises** outside the commune let peasants sell surplus
    - Foreign investment flowed in with eased capital barriers
- Funded infrastructure, purchase of US debt, and the **Belt and Road Initiative** (competes with Western development institutions)
- Global supply chains restructured, manufacturing relocated to China, East Asia, some Global South states
- Digital race with Silicon Valley
    - Weibo, Alibaba, Tencent, Huawei; state ensures harmony of interests with "private" firms
    - A "Shenzhen Valley" is one of the strongest arguments for China as the centre of a new order

#### The Chinese model of the state

- Authoritarian state and Xi's "hero-of-history" mystique are _salient features_ of a foundation of power, not morbid symptoms
- An "off-the-shelf" model: illiberal government plus high growth
    - Appeals to leaders hostile to Western paradigms and looking for a sponsor
- Seen as a **developmental state** that can level inequalities liberalism produced
    - **SOEs**: balance profitability with political goals of the state
    - Arrighi: the key reform was exposing SOEs to _competition_, not privatization
    - SOEs act like private firms to attract foreign capital but deploy it at the state's command
    - Township and Village Enterprises balance local autonomy and state-managed growth
- Others have noticed
    - BRI: Southeast, South, and Central Asia, Middle East, Africa, Eastern Europe
    - New Development Bank ("BRICS" Bank): nondollar development assistance on different terms from Bretton Woods institutions
    - Boosters outside China see its model as a rejoinder to Western liberalism

#### The Pax Sinica

- Military trending toward parity with the US and its alliance network
    - Regional hegemon in Asia, with an irredentist agenda (Hong Kong, Xinjiang, Taiwan)
    - African military base; stakeholder in the "scramble for the Arctic"
    - Tensions: Diaoyu Islands (also claimed by Japan), South China Sea (four other claimants)
    - Spending from under $1B after the civil war to $175B in 2019; nuclear stockpiles, aircraft carriers
- Buildup alone isn't a Pax Sinica; would also need
    1. Network of allies taking security cues from China (it has the SCO, but no pluralistic security community of close allies)
    2. Willingness to manage and sponsor the order, plus moral zeal to expand it (as the US did)
- If it happens, it would look very different from the US-sponsored order

### Bright Lights, Dark Age: The New Medievalism

- Middle Ages: rival claims to rule
    - Holy Roman Empire and Catholic Church claimed universal authority; French and English monarchies asserted local authority
    - Led to events like the French Revolution, which ended that assemblage and began the interregnum that built the liberal capitalist order
- A slip back into a "stale and sclerotic" order is possible
- **Neomedievalism** (Hedley Bull): states share authority with regional/world bodies and substate authorities until sovereignty stops being applicable
    - Interregnum isn't from one hegemon to another (US to China), but to _diffused sovereignty_ at local, national, and global levels
    - States still claim de jure sovereignty and have de facto sovereignty, but it's hedged, limited, or dissolved

#### Neomedieval social relations of production

- Multilevel fragmentation, especially in digital spaces controlled by a few **tech-lords**
- A _consumerist peasantry_ subject to the lords of the digital space
    - Labour shifts from food to information and images
    - McKenzie Wark: image production is in-sourced to unpaid leisure labour; culture industries are now "vulture industries"
- Silicon Valley and Shenzhen "Valley" lords have sovereign power over their spaces
    - Peasants "harvest" text, images, video, data, code; cyberlords sell it
    - Ex. Bezos (Amazon), Zuckerberg (Facebook), Musk (X)
- Digital goods don't meet material needs, so precarity is high: the **"gig and grift"** economy
    - **Gig**: virtual networks, independent contractors, low wages, no benefits, pay only when actively working
        - Ex. Uber, Bolt, Airbnb, DoorDash, Grubhub, Just Eat, Turo, Virtuo
        - US gig workers up 10M in 2021 to ~57M
    - **Grift**: income depends on persuading the public to give donations, subscriptions, or ad revenue
        - Ex. YouTube, Facebook, TikTok creators; influencers (influencer marketing ~$10B in 2020)
        - Professionals too (Substack writers, Patreon/Spotify podcasters, artists, novelists)
        - Ex. OnlyFans gives sex workers more autonomy

#### Sovereign institutions in neomedievalism

- Pooled sovereignty (UN, EU) originally still derived from consenting states
- Later trade pacts (NAFTA, TPP) shifted sovereign power toward private actors
    - **ISDS tribunals**: investors can sue states over nationalization or laws (ex. environmental regulation)
    - Decided by technocratic "judges" who rotate between suing governments and judging

#### Neomedieval world order

- Corporations and banks have de facto sovereignty
    - Tech-lords of Silicon Valley
    - Global finance: IMF and World Bank dictated policy to developing nations; after 2008 this extended to advanced states
    - Ex. Greece's troika again
- Despite Brexit, push for more EU power (immigration, environment, defence), but toward a neomedieval form rather than a "united states of Europe"

### Postcapitalism

- Earlier models are mostly reactionary mutations of liberal power (the most plausible given current capabilities)
- Post-2008 left surge
    - Majority of young US millennials fine identifying as socialist
    - Greece: left party elected promising to defy austerity
    - Spain: Podemos and Ciudadanos
    - US: Bernie Sanders (2016, 2020); party elites consolidated centrists behind Biden after Sanders won Nevada
- A socialist world order of worker-controlled states is implausible near-term
- But what would an assemblage not driven by capitalist principles look like? (_postcapitalist_ doesn't mean socialist or communist)

#### Postcapital social relations of production

- Most lucrative value is now **informational assets** and IP: patents, code, algorithms, formulae, brands, media, and the digital network itself
- The **digital worker** becomes the agent of change (not the industrial worker)
    - Drucker: the "universal educated person" at an office, lab, or computer
- Integrated knowledge networks let the knowledgeable and motivated self-organize with little supervision
    - Cheapens IP: Creative Commons, open-source resources (Wikipedia), 3D printing
- Networks also transform political action
    - Ex. Arab Spring, London riots, George Floyd protests
    - Ex. Mohammed Bouazizi's self-immolation spread through Facebook and YouTube and helped topple Tunisia's regime

#### The state in postcapitalism

- Shifts from maximizing economic assets to maximizing **social assets**
    - Ex. environment: abandon the market for a more aggressive regulatory stance, but harness knowledge networks to reach zero carbon
    - Others: stabilize finance and eliminate sovereign debt, raise human development, improve education and healthcare, reduce compulsory labour, **universal basic income**
- **Varoufakis**
    - UBI financed by taxing tech companies that profit from users' data
    - Private capital assets (incl. land) treated as monopoly control, abolished for communal management
    - Central banks pay money directly to citizens instead of serving big investment firms
    - Blockchain-style common ledger
    - Migration managed globally: anyone can leave, but states have a say in who enters and how many

#### Postcapitalist world order

- Security shifts from managing inter-state conflict to **human security** (biosphere, refugees, food insecurity)
    - Interstate conflict still happens, but mediation by intergovernmental and nongovernmental mechanisms is encouraged; neutral states broker ceasefires
    - Force justified by _responsibility to protect_ more than territorial integrity (despite Iraq and Ukraine invasions)
    - Fewer NATO-style pacts that depend on one superpower
- Biggest change in trade and finance governance
    - Varoufakis's **international green new deal**: massive investment program around a digital International Clearing Union
    - Digital IMF currency unit for all international payments; national currencies float against it
    - Wealth fund built from trade surpluses and deficits
    - World Bank channels idle savings into green investments
- Far from the communist international Gramsci's contemporaries imagined, but a world where money and military power aren't the core of the institutional architecture

### Conclusion

- Caesars rarely solve the governing impasse (Gramsci)
    - Eventually give way to either a restored mutation of the old order or a new, more progressive configuration with a new historic bloc
    - Current Caesars will fade or survive marginally (ex. Kim Jong-Un)
- Four sketches (countless others are possible)
    1. **Empire**: liberal order becomes a pseudo-sovereign "hyperliberal" entity; Caesarism is states and strongmen reclaiming lost power but too embedded to resist assimilation
    2. **Chinese hegemony**: authoritarianism stabilized inside a liberal capitalist arrangement
        - Xi's Caesarism as _Augustus_, not Julius: a stable, permanent structure (the Roman Empire)
    3. **Neomedievalism**: eroding state sovereignty plus rising power of banks, legal institutions, and billionaire barons
        - New Caesars claim splintered sovereignty
    4. **Postcapitalism**: an _emancipatory_ order that doesn't equate growth and profit with human prosperity
- Gramsci on fascism: Caesars are a bid by vested interests of the old order to cling to power as legitimacy fades
- Paralysis must be overcome first; Caesarism has been resilient and may last years or decades
    - Reasons explored in the book's conclusion
