## Intro
- Complex modern issues are cross-border
- Regional organizations can use combined resources, knowledge, and a unified front to address global challenges.
## Historical Context
#### Rise of Nation States
- Treaty of Westphalia ended the Thirty Years' Ware in 1648
	- Gives rise to modern nation-state
- WWI/II and decolonization reinforce nation states
- Strong ability to adapt to changes
- States are the fundamental actors in international relations
#### Evolution of Regionalism
- Modern idea of regionalism was forged in the 20/21st centuries
- European Coal and Steel Community (1951) preceded the EU
- Spike in regional integration post-1945
## Theoretical Framework of Regionalism
#### Realist
- Regionalism motivated by states strategic interests
	- Maximize power/security
- Ex. NATO keeping North Atlantic region stable
- Deter foreign attack
- Sense of security amongst members
#### Liberal
- Importance of institutions and collaboration internationally
- Encourage economic interdependence
- Political cooperation
- Social integration
- Ex. EU
	- High levels of integration/cooperation/cohesion
	- Single market/customs/currency
	- Significant economic stability and growth
#### Constructivist
- Identities/norms/ideas determine regionalism
- Socially constructed identities and values between states
- Ex. ASEAN
	- Censuses
	- Non-interference norm
- Regional unity and mutual respect
- Common identities
