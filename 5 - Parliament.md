## 5.1 The Role of Parliament
- Canada's Parliament = **House of Commons** + **Senate** + **Crown** (CA 1867, s. 17)
- Only the **House of Commons** is democratic; plays predominant role by convention
- Constitutional foundation: "Constitution Similar in Principle to That of the United Kingdom"
- Historical evolution:
    - Originally advisory body to Crown ("parler" = to speak)
    - Advice became binding → Parliament controlled legislative power
    - **Responsible government**: executive must have confidence of Parliament
    - By 19th century: "golden age" of Parliament (most important institution)
- **Decline of Parliament's power**:
    - Rise of disciplined political parties → power shifted to cabinet
    - Party discipline gives cabinet control over Parliament's law-making
    - Modern role: **make cabinet accountable to public**
        - Opposition criticizes government, offers alternatives
        - Scrutiny damages governing party's election chances
- **Minority governments**: Parliament plays more important role
    - Governments must compromise to avoid elections
    - But real decisions still made by parties, not House debates
## 5.2 The Parliamentary Calendar

- **Parliament numbering**: Tied to elections (1st in 1867, 43rd in 2019)
- **Convening**: PM decides timing, GG convenes on PM's advice
- **Sessions**:
    - No set length; end via **prorogation** (PM advises GG)
    - Legislative work must be completed in its session or restart
    - Typically ~1 year each; ~4 sessions per Parliament
    - Trudeau's first term: one session (2015-19)
- **Sittings**: Each meeting of House of Commons
    - Can be long or brief (even one day)
    - **Adjournments** between sittings (overnight or weeks)
- **Session start**: **Throne speech** (GG reads, PMO prepares)
    - Outlines government's legislative program
    - Debated and voted on (**confidence vote**)
- **Session end**: PM requests **dissolution** → general election
- Fixed election legislation provides timing clarity, but confidence votes can force early elections
## 5.3 The House of Commons: Membership and Officers
- **Membership**: 338 MPs, each representing a **riding** (electoral district)
- **Riding allocation**:
    - Based on **representation by population**
    - Deviations:
        - Section 51A: provinces get at least as many seats as Senate seats
        - Territories: 1 MP each (overrepresented)
    - Adjusted after each decennial census
- **MP categories**:
    - Constitutional: **cabinet members** vs **private members**
    - Political: **government MPs** vs **opposition MPs**
- **Official Opposition**:
    - Largest opposition party
    - Privileges: special speaking rights, consulted on appointments, **Stornoway** residence
    - Role: criticize government while remaining loyal to Crown
- **Shadow cabinet**: opposition critics for each ministry
- **House layout**:
    - Government and opposition face each other
    - **Front benches**: cabinet and shadow cabinet
    - **Backbenchers**: MPs behind front benches
- **Parliamentary officers** (neutral):
    - **Speaker**: elected by House, presides over debates, maintains impartiality
        - Votes only to break ties; convention: vote to maintain status quo
    - **Clerk of the House**: official paperwork, procedural advice
    - **Sergeant-at-arms**: security, ceremonial role
    - **Hansard**: official record of debates
- **Other officers** (report to Parliament):
    - **Auditor General**: reviews government spending (e.g., sponsorship scandal)
    - **Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO)**: independent financial assessments (e.g., F-35 cost estimates)
    - **Conflict of Interest and Ethics Officer**
    - Chief Electoral Officer, Commissioner of Official Languages, Privacy Commissioner

## 5.4 The Business of the House of Commons

- **Legislative process**:
    - **Government bills**: introduced by cabinet
    - **Private members' bills**: introduced by non-cabinet MPs
    - Stages: **first reading**, **second reading**, **report stage**, **third reading**, **royal assent**, **proclamation**
- **Financial scrutiny**:
    - **Scrutiny of public expenditure**
    - **Estimates**: government spending plans
    - **Resolutions**: non-binding expressions of opinion

## 5.8 The Senate

- **Purpose**: chamber of **"sober second thought"**
- **Original intents**:
    1. Protect **property rights** (senators must have $4,000+ assets)
    2. Represent **less populous regions** (regional equality)
- **Composition**: 105 seats
    - Ontario: 24, Quebec: 24, Western Canada: 24 (6 per province)
    - Maritimes: 24, Newfoundland: 6, Territories: 1 each
- **Appointment**: by GG on PM's advice (section 24, CA 1867)
- **Powers**: nearly equal to House of Commons in law
    - Exceptions: House leads on **money bills** (s. 53, CA 1867)
    - Senate has only suspensive veto on constitutional amendments (s. 47(1), CA 1982)
- **Practice**: Conventionally defers to House of Commons
    - Typically makes recommendations/amendments, not outright rejection
    - But has occasionally asserted itself:
        - Free trade bill (1988): Liberal Senate blocked PC government bill
        - GST bill (1991): Liberal Senate threatened to block; Mulroney appointed 8 extra PC senators via s. 26
- **Role today**: reviews legislation, cleans up errors, provides regional perspective

---

## 5.9 Senate Reform

- **Dissatisfaction**: long-standing, Senate seen as undemocratic, wasteful, or lobby for big business
- **Reform options**:
    1. **Abolition**: simple but overlooks useful functions
    2. **Election**: give democratic legitimacy, better represent regions (like US Senate)
    3. **Status quo**: useful for cleaning up errors, cabinet representation, "promoting" ministers
- **Recent developments**:
    - Harper: proposed limited terms, provincial consultation → Supreme Court ruled requires constitutional amendment (2014)
    - Harper stopped appointing senators after ruling
    - Trudeau: appointed **independent senators**, created **Independent Advisory Board for Senate Appointments**
    - **Independent Senators Group (ISG)**: largest group, votes consistently with Liberals
    - New Senate: more active, assertive, deliberative while respecting House primacy
