# Chapter 6: Elections

## 6.1 Elections and Representation
- Canada is a **representative democracy**, not direct democracy
	- Central democratic mechanism = elections
- **Section 3 of CA 1982:** Every citizen has right to vote and be qualified for membership in House/Legislative Assembly
- **Democratic accountability under responsible government:**
	- MPs directly accountable (must be periodically re-elected)
	- PM and cabinet indirectly accountable (must maintain House confidence)
	- Elections to House = democratic linchpin of regime

### Three Models of Representation
1. **Delegate:** Spokesperson who faithfully transmits views of majority of constituents
2. **Trustee:** Elected for character/judgment, free to decide how to vote on each issue
3. **Party Member:** Task is to be loyal supporter of party policies

- **Which model does Canada use?**
	- Primarily **party representation**
	- Parties establish link between voter and cabinet
	- Creates concept of **mandate**: Party that forms government can claim electorate endorsed their policy line
	- Strongest link between electorate and government
	- MPs can act as delegates/trustees in caucus debates, but expected to vote party line publicly
- **Evidence:** Canadians seldom elect independents (MPs without party affiliation)

### Floor-Crossing
- **Example: David Emerson (2006)**
	- Elected as Liberal in Vancouver Kingsway
	- Immediately crossed floor to join Conservative caucus, became minister in Harper government
	- Voters outraged: Violated "party member" theory of representation
	- Consistent with "trustee" theory (argued Vancouver needed voice in cabinet)
- **General pattern:** Canadians punish floor-crossers by voting them out
	- Indicates party representation is most fundamental aspect
	- Ex. Eve Adams (2015): Crossed from Conservative to Liberal, couldn't even win Liberal nomination

## 6.2 Representation and Diversity
- **Microcosm Theory of Representation:** Legislative bodies fully representative only if assembly is microcosm of society
	- If 50% of population is female → roughly 50% of representatives should be female
	- Ethnic groups should be proportionally represented
- **Arguments for microcosm theory:**
	1. **Fairness/inclusivity:** Lack of representation suggests discrimination, undermines perceived legitimacy
	2. **Diverse perspectives:** Ensures all viewpoints heard, interests defended/advanced
- **Application in Canada:**
	- PM forms cabinet reflecting geographic, religious, ethnic, language, gender diversity
	- Increasingly influential for Supreme Court appointments
	- Some discussion of electoral reform to mirror social composition
		- Ex. Charlottetown Accord proposed half Senate seats reserved for women

### Progress on Gender Representation
- Women have been party leaders at provincial/national levels
- Kim Campbell served as PM
- Slow but steady increase in percentage of women MPs:
	- 1980: 5% → 2019: 29%
- **Strategies to increase women's representation:**
	1. Multi-member electoral districts (parties would balance slate with men/women)
	2. Party list PR (parties could compose balanced lists)
	3. Work within existing system (find more women candidates)
		- Liberal Party under Dion: 35% women candidates target (2006)
		- Problem: National targets require central interference with local nomination rights
		- **Token candidate problem:** Parties nominate women in unwinnable ridings
			- Ex. 2008: 37% of Liberal candidates were women, but only 24% of winners were women
- **Trudeau's 2015 cabinet:** Gender parity principle = significant advance
	- With more women in cabinet → more likely to win party nominations → higher numbers of female MPs

## 6.3 Single-Member Plurality (SMP) System
- **Three main factors in electoral system design:**
	1. **Electoral districts:** Divided into districts vs. single national election?
	2. **Number of representatives:** How many MPs? Balance representation vs. functionality
	3. **Method of electing:** Plurality vs. majority? Choose candidate vs. rank preferences?

### Canada's SMP System
- Adopted from UK model, with federal seat allocation like U.S.
- **Single-Member Plurality (SMP):**
	- Country divided into geographically distinct electoral districts (ridings/constituencies)
	- Each district has one representative
	- Winning candidate gains most votes (plurality, not necessarily majority)
	- Often called "first-past-the-post" system

### Seat Distribution
- **Section 37 of CA 1867:** Specifies MPs per province
- **Sections 51-52:** Readjustment after each decennial census to reflect population changes
	- 2011 Census → Fair Representation Act added 30 seats (mostly Ontario, BC, Alberta)
- **Section 51A protection:** No province can have fewer MPs than senators
	- PEI guaranteed 4 MPs (population deserves only 1)
	- New Brunswick must have 10 MPs (population warrants only 7)
- **Result:** Smaller provinces overrepresented

### Electoral Boundaries
- **Independent electoral boundaries commissions** (one per province)
	- Three-person commission chaired by judge
	- High-profile impartial citizens
- **Considerations:**
	1. **Community of interest:** Keep minority populations together in one riding (ensures voice heard)
	2. **Electoral quotient:** Average population per district (2011: 111,166)
		- Impossible to make every constituency exactly equal
		- Remote areas may have smaller populations (too large geographically otherwise)
	3. **Federalism effect:** Smaller provinces with guaranteed minimums have below-quotient districts

## 6.4 Voting in Canada
- **Dissolving Parliament and calling elections:**
	- Governor General dissolves on PM's "advice"
	- Minority government: House can force election via confidence vote
	- Majority government: PM traditionally had free hand

### Election Timing
- **Section 4(1) of CA 1982:** No Parliament lasts longer than 5 years
- **Expectation:** Elections in fourth year (waiting 5 years = sign government afraid to face electorate)
- **Fixed-date legislation (Harper government):**
	- Elections every fourth year, third Monday in October
	- **Problem:** Responsible government makes truly fixed dates impossible
		- House can express lack of confidence anytime
		- Must restore democratic legitimacy via new elections
	- **Loophole:** Legislation didn't revoke Governor General's power to dissolve Parliament early
		- Harper used this in September 2008 (13 months before fixed date)
		- House hadn't declared lack of confidence
		- Harper arranged timing most favourable to his party (exactly what legislation supposed to prevent)
- **2018 legal changes:** Campaigns minimum 36 days, maximum 50 days
	- Harper's 2015 campaign was 78 days (to suppress critical third-party advertising, take advantage of fundraising edge)

### Election Administration
- **Chief Electoral Officer:** Politically neutral officer of Parliament
	- Appoints returning officer for each of 338 electoral districts
- **Polls:** Electoral districts divided into smaller units (~250 voters each)
	- Deputy returning officer and poll clerks run election at each poll
- **Enumerators:** Two per poll, prepare official voters list
- **Voting eligibility:** All citizens 18+ (except Chief Electoral Officer)
	- Returning officers vote only in case of tie
- **Candidate nomination:**
	- 100 eligible voters' signatures required
	- $1,000 deposit (refunded if candidate gets 10%+ of vote)
	- Candidates don't have to be residents of constituency (but politically disadvantageous)

## 6.5 Public Opinion Polling and Electoral Choice
- Polling is pervasive in modern democratic politics
- **Why so common?**
	- Useful tool for mass marketing
	- Interest groups, government agencies, parties use polls to determine:
		- What issues important to public
		- Whether particular idea/policy/personality could prove popular
- **Argument for polling:** Resource for modern democracy
	- Governments/parties need to know what people thinking
	- Can give people what they want via polls

### Concerns About Polling
1. **Reduced role of MPs:** Polls replace MP as source of information about voters/issues
	- Not coincidental that as polls proliferated, MPs lost prominence relative to party leader
2. **Promotes conformism:** Something good simply because majority in poll thinks it good
	- Tocqueville: "Moral empire of the majority" = theory of equality applied to intellects
	- Danger: Believe something true/good because majority believes it
		- Often realized with racial/religious intolerance issues

### Pre-Election Polls - Specific Concerns
1. **Horse-race coverage:** Media focuses on who's ahead vs. what issues are
	- Politics treated as sporting event, trivialized
	- Voters pay attention to who's winning vs. who ought to win
2. **Veneer of objectivity used for partisan purposes:** Polling questions can be "leading"
	- Worded to lead respondent to answers fitting client's bias
3. **Effect on voting behavior:**
	- **Strategic voting:** Voters switch from badly trailing candidates to leading contenders
	- **Bandwagon effect:** Voters want winning candidate from party likely to form government
	- Result: SMP system's difficulty for third parties magnified
- **Fundamental tension:** Prohibiting poll publication violates freedom of press
	- But if polls diminish thoughtful participation → anti-democratic
	- Reveals tension between devotion to liberalism vs. democracy

## 6.6 Effects of SMP
- SMP divides voting into 338 separate elections
- Smaller provinces constitutionally overrepresented
- **Key effect:** Substantial discrepancy between party's share of votes vs. share of seats

### Example: Ontario 2019
- Conservative: 33% of vote → 30% of seats
- Liberal: 41.5% of vote → 65% of seats
- NDP: 17% of vote → 5% of seats

### Four Major Effects of SMP
1. **Rewards largest national party** with more seats than it "deserves"
	- Frequently converts minority of votes into majority of seats
	- Ex. 1987 New Brunswick: Liberals won 60% of vote → won ALL 58 seats
		- Conservatives/NDP won ~40% of votes → 0 seats
	- Only 3 of 25 post-WWII elections gave single party >50% of votes (1940, 1958, 1984)
	- Yet 17 of those elections resulted in majority governments
	- Harper (2011) and Trudeau (2015): Majority governments with slightly <40% of votes
	- 2008: Conservatives got 46.4% of seats with only 37.6% of vote
2. **Handicaps smaller national parties** (NDP, Green)
	- Smaller share of seats than share of popular vote
	- 2019: Green won 6.5% of vote but only 3 seats (should be ~20)
	- NDP: 16% of vote → only 7% of seats
3. **Encourages regionalism by fostering regional parties**
	- Bloc: 7.7% of total vote (vs. NDP's 15.9%) but won 32 seats (vs. NDP's 24)
	- Bloc's support concentrated entirely in Quebec
4. **Magnifies regional strengths/weaknesses of national parties**
	- 2019: Liberals shut out of Alberta/Saskatchewan despite attracting 13.7% and 11.5% of votes

## 6.7 Proportional Representation (PR)
- Used in many European liberal democracies
- **Party List System (pure PR):** Used in Israel, Netherlands
	- Each party prepares ranked list of candidates
	- Electoral districts abolished
	- Each province retains seats, awarded based on proportion of popular vote
	- Electorate votes for parties, not candidates
	- Seats allocated by proportion of vote received

### Example: Ontario 2019
- **Actual SMP result:** Liberal 79, Conservative 36, NDP 6, Green 0
- **If PR used:** Liberal 55, Conservative 40, NDP 19, Green 7

### Arguments FOR PR
- **Fairer than SMP:** Parties get seats proportional to total vote distribution
	- Doesn't "distort" electorate's voting pattern
	- Doesn't favour certain party types over others
- **More diverse representation within each party caucus:**
	- Party leadership can structure lists to ensure representation for traditionally under-represented groups (women, visible minorities, Indigenous peoples)
- **National representation example (2019):**
	- SMP actual: Liberal 157, Conservative 121, NDP 24, Green 3, BQ 32
	- If PR used: Liberal 112, Conservative 116, NDP 54, Green 22, BQ 26
	- Smaller national parties (NDP, Green) would win far more seats

### Arguments AGAINST PR
1. **Political instability:**
	- Canadian electorate rarely gives any party majority of votes
	- SMP transforms plurality into majority of seats
	- PR would make majority governments extremely rare
	- Minority/coalition governments (generally less stable) would become norm
2. **More divisive politics:**
	- Facilitates emergence of small, single-issue parties and parties with non-mainstream agendas
	- Currently shut out by SMP (must come first in riding to win)
	- Under PR, such parties could gain seats with small percentage of vote
	- In minority government, small party could dictate terms to party in power ("tail wags dog")
	- Proportional representation of parties ≠ proportional representation of political power
3. **Loss of local representation:**
	- Local voting districts would no longer have own MP
	- Ontario would likely be dominated by Toronto representatives
	- MPs would owe positions to party bosses in Ottawa (who put names high on list)
	- Not to local electorate
	- Makes MPs less responsive to electorate, more subordinate to party bosses
	- Variation: Allow voters to indicate preferences for candidates on list (rearrange ranking)

### Mixed Member Proportional (MMP)
- **Blends party list PR with SMP**
	- Used in New Zealand, Germany
	- Some seats elected via party list (usually 1/4 to 1/2)
	- Rest elected using SMP
	- Voters vote twice: once for local candidate, once for party list
- Addresses loss of local representation problem

## 6.8 Alternative Voting (AV)
- Also known as **preferential ballot**
- Used in Ireland, Australia, Malta, France (presidential election)
- Canadian parties increasingly use for leadership elections

### Three Implementation Methods
1. **Majority Runoff Election**
	- Candidate must win majority (50% + 1) to win seat
	- If no majority: Runoff election with only top two candidates
	- Winner certain to have majority support
2. **Ranked Ballot (Preferential Ballot)**
	- Compressed runoff
	- Voter ranks candidates in order of preference
	- If no candidate has majority of first-place votes:
		- Second choices from lowest-placed candidate added to totals
		- Repeated until someone has majority
	- Accomplishes same as runoff but without need for second election
	- Runoff costlier but voters have time to deliberate on second choice
3. **Single Transferable Vote (STV)**
	- Preferential voting used with multi-member district system
	- Provides more proportional division of seats
	- District with 5 seats: Each party can nominate up to 5 candidates
	- Voters rank candidates in order of preference
	- Candidates reaching quota (~20% in 5-member district) are elected
	- More proportional than SMP: If Party A and Party B have equal support, they split seats instead of winner-take-all

## 6.9 Electoral Reform
- Historically academic issue, raised by:
	- Supporters of parties disadvantaged by SMP
	- People troubled by "wasted votes" for losing party
	- People who think seat percentage should match popular vote percentage
- Traditionally ignored by parties in power (they benefit from SMP)

### Provincial Reform Efforts
- PEI, New Brunswick, Ontario, Quebec expressed interest in MMP
	- No change yet occurred
- **PEI:** Narrowly supported MMP in 2016 referendum
	- Low turnout, premier wanted second expression of support
	- Put to voters again 2019: Substantial support but not enough for change
- **Ontario:** Rejected electoral reform in 2007
- **British Columbia:** Considered multi-member STV in 2005
	- 58% voted in favour
	- Not adopted (legislation required 60% support for major change)
	- Second referendum 2009: Defeated, fell short of majority

### Federal Reform Effort (Trudeau 2015)
- Trudeau declared 2015 election would be Canada's last under SMP
- **House of Commons Special Committee on Electoral Reform (May 2016):**
	- Members from all parties
	- Held consultations across country
	- Government favoured preferential ballot
	- **Committee's main recommendations:**
		1. Implement modified version of PR
		2. Put final proposal to national referendum
- **Trudeau government's response:**
	- No strong consensus on reform type
	- Difficult to formulate proposal and hold referendum with clear question
	- Abandoned reform agenda
	- Trudeau criticized for breaking election promise

### Partisan Self-Interest in Electoral Systems
- **Conservatives:** Favor SMP (allowed majority 2011-15 with <40% votes), oppose PR and especially STV
- **Liberals:** Favor STV (2015 election: Would have won 224 seats = 66% with <40% votes)
	- STV might make Liberals near-permanent government (center party = likely second choice)
	- Would settle for PR (makes majority difficult but Liberals likely in any coalition)
- **NDP and Greens:** Suffer under SMP, strongly promote PR (would provide many more seats)
- **Bloc:** Benefits from SMP in most elections, would not do as well under other systems
	- But PR might give strategic advantage (minority governments = more leverage for concessions)

### 2015 Election Projected Results
- **SMP:** Liberal 184, Conservative 99, NDP 44, BQ 10, Green 1
- **PR:** Liberal 134, Conservative 109, NDP 67, BQ 16, Green 12
- **STV:** Liberal 224, Conservative 61, NDP 50, BQ 2, Green 1
