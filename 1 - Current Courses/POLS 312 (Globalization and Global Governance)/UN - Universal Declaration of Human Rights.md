# Universal Declaration of Human Rights: Summary

## Preamble
- Recognizes the inherent dignity and equal rights of all people as the foundation of freedom, justice, and peace
- Notes that disregard for human rights led to atrocities, and that rights should be protected by the rule of law
- Calls the Declaration a common standard for all peoples and nations, to be promoted through teaching and education
## Foundational principles (Articles 1-2)
- All people are born free and equal in dignity and rights
- Rights apply to everyone without distinction (race, sex, religion, opinion, origin, status, etc.)
## Life, liberty, and security (Articles 3-5)
- Right to life, liberty, and personal security
- No slavery or servitude
- No torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment
## Law and justice (Articles 6-11)
- Recognition as a person before the law and equal protection under it
- Effective remedy for rights violations
- No arbitrary arrest, detention, or exile
- Right to a fair, public hearing by an independent tribunal
- Presumption of innocence; no retroactive criminal penalties
## Privacy, movement, and belonging (Articles 12-15)
- Protection from arbitrary interference with privacy, family, home, and correspondence
- Freedom of movement, and the right to leave and return to one's country
- Right to seek asylum from persecution
- Right to a nationality
## Family and property (Articles 16-17)
- Right to marry and found a family, with free and full consent
- Family is entitled to protection by society and the State
- Right to own property and not be arbitrarily deprived of it
## Thought, expression, and participation (Articles 18-21)
- Freedom of thought, conscience, and religion (including the right to change it)
- Freedom of opinion and expression
- Freedom of peaceful assembly and association
- Right to take part in government and to genuine, periodic elections with universal suffrage
## Economic, social, and cultural rights (Articles 22-27)
- Social security and the rights needed for dignity
- Right to work, equal pay, fair conditions, and to join trade unions
- Rest, leisure, and paid holidays
- Adequate standard of living (food, clothing, housing, medical care)
- Right to education (free at the elementary level; parents choose the kind of education for their children)
- Right to participate in cultural life and to have one's creative work protected
## Limits and safeguards (Articles 28-30)
- Everyone is entitled to a social and international order where these rights can be realized
- Everyone has duties to the community
- Rights may only be limited by law to protect others' rights and meet the needs of morality, public order, and general welfare
- Rights cannot be used to destroy other rights, and nothing in the Declaration permits that
