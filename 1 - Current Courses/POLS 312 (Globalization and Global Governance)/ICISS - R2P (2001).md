# Synopsis
- Several principles for responsible intervention:
1. **Just Cause**
	- Exceptional and outstanding reasoning
		a. Large scale loss of life
		b. Large scale ethnic cleansing
2. **Precautionary Principles**
	a. Right intention - Primary purpose is to halt/avert suffering
	b. Last resort - All non-military options must be explored first
	c. Proportional means - Scale/duration/intensity should be the minimum necessary
	d. Reasonable prospects - A reasonable chance of success in the operation
3. **Right Authority**
	- Gain authority from the UN Security Council
	- Authorization should be sought before any military intervention is carried out
	- Security Council should deal with requests quickly
	- The P5 should agree not to veto unnecessarily, or for obstructive reasons
	- If the Security Council rejects or doesn't deal with a proposal fast enough:
		- Consider the matter at the general assembly in emergency session
		- Action is subject the the jurisdiction of the region
	- Security council must account in its deliberations for the case that it may fail to discharge its responsibility correctly
4. **Operational Principles**
	- Clear objectives
	- Unified approach between states
	- Do not attempt to defeat a state, but rather protect its population
	- Adhere to rules of engagement and intl humanitarian law
	- Accept that force protection cannot become the principle objective
	- Maximize collaboration with humanitarian organizations
# Chapter 1. The Policy Challenge
- Rwanda (1994) was a major failure
	- UN forces were present
	- Security council knew of genocide planning
	- Credible strategies for mitigation were available
	- Security Council refused to take necessary action
- Kosovo, 5 years later, did have an intervention
	- Lots of questioning as to whether the cause was just
		- Was it for selfish purposes
		- Were peaceful means exhausted?
		- Acting without permission from UN or Security Council
	- NATO intervened, and a possible bloody civil war may have been averted
- Bosnia was another shortcoming
	- Massacre in Srebrenica in 1995
	- Supposed to be a 'UN safe area'
		- No real military backing
		- 8,000 Bosniak killed
	- Betrayal of a promise of protection
- Each of the four massacres (adding Somalia) occurred at a time when it was expected for more collaboration, post Cold War
- Challenging to balance between those who say the international community is not intervening enough and those who say it is too often
	- Others take issue over legality, process, precedent, etc.
