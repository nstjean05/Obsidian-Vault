#AISummary 
### Chapter 8: Kingdom Ambassadors in a Foreign Land (Ed Stetzer, _Christians in the Age of Outrage_)
- Opening stats
    - Politics are extremely important to 30% of evangelicals vs 18% of non-evangelicals
    - 67% of evangelicals agree a Christian can benefit from a political leader whose personal life doesn't match Christian teaching
#### Opening story: the Nashville church
- Stetzer served as interim pastor of a 9,000-member Southern church in the early 2000s, then was asked to help a second church in transition
    - Public, ugly conflict peaked at a Mother's Day 2009 meeting; the pastor left
    - Dropped from a megachurch to ~600 people in a 3,000-seat sanctuary (10% full), with finances in shambles
    - Infighting made the local front page 5 times and national news once, i.e. airing Christian outrage instead of preaching the gospel
- He took no salary, to avoid distraction and keep freedom to make decisions
    - Let a couple dozen staff go; church grew to ~1,000 in a year
- New message: "get back to the basics" and be known for what Jesus was known for (showing and sharing the good news)
- **Nashville flood** (May 1, 2010, during a "servolution" service day)
    - 13.5 inches of rain in 48 hours; Cumberland River crested over 50 ft; 21 killed in Tennessee
    - Church (on a hill across from Opryland) became an evacuation site for the hotel and a city command centre
        - Hosted two Grand Ole Opry performances
    - Members mucked out houses, handed out water and school supplies; Stetzer became a de facto chaplain
    - First Sunday after was Mother's Day again, the one-year anniversary of the worst moment
- Before: fighting, self-preservation, outrage used "like a weapon"; after: known for showing and sharing Jesus' love
- Key question: **What are we known for in the community? What do we represent?**
### Becoming Ambassadors to the Age of Outrage
- Last chapter was _what_ we believe (worldview); this one is _who we are_
- Christians no longer represent the dominant culture, so they must rethink cultural engagement, mission, and evangelism in a post-Christian society
- Key text: 2 Corinthians 5:18-21
    - God reconciled us through Christ and gave us the _ministry of reconciliation_; "we are ambassadors for Christ"
    - Everything we do starts from the reality that Christ already did the reconciling (Ephesians 2:1)
    - We don't just receive grace; we participate in extending it to the unreconciled (lost, purposeless, lonely)
- **Ambassadors in Paul's time**
    - Rome sent armies, not ambassadors; other countries sent ambassadors to entreat Rome toward peace (Martyn Percy)
    - Ad hoc representative with a particular task who returns when it's done, with an _appeal_ at the core
    - Bash's four functions (Roman Empire)
        1. Specific task to accomplish
        2. Travels to a specific place
        3. Dedicated to the interests of the sender
        4. Doesn't return until the task is done
    - More than messengers or heralds: representatives with a mission who live in the foreign land
        - Study culture, learn the language, eat the food, build bridges
- Christians study the culture and see how its mechanisms oppose the Kingdom, while having God-given _compassion_ for people far from home
- Three ways to understand our "sentness"
    1. Sent with allegiance to the King
    2. Sent with a message of reconciliation
    3. Sent to a foreign land with a mission to complete
#### 1. Sent by a King
- Being an ambassador speaks to God's nature: he isn't distant, he's on a mission of reconciliation
    - **Missio Dei** (mission of God)
    - We are a _sent people_; mission is participating in God's "sending love" (David Bosch)
- Paul calls himself a _servant_ of Christ (and James does too), a title of honour, not self-deprecation
    - Their authority wasn't their own brilliance or leadership strategies
    - Mission is "the privileged participation of the servants in the Master's grand plan"
- Ex. Benedict Arnold: swore allegiance but lacked "embodied fealty"
- The age of outrage has endless temptations for our allegiance
    - Ultimate allegiance is only to Jesus; when people or causes step out of line with the King's will, we speak out as his representatives
- Romans 15:20: ambition is to preach where Christ hasn't been named
#### 2. Sent with a message of reconciliation
- Church's mission is defined: the ministry and message of reconciliation, with no room for debate
- Ephesians 2: four truths
    1. **Hostility and division** between God and man (2:1-3)
    2. God is the **initiator** (2:4-5)
    3. Christ is the **sole solution** (2:5-9, by grace through faith)
    4. Reconciliation is the **beginning of transformation**, not the end; we're set on mission to help reconcile others (2:10)
- Message is transformative and personal: God reconciled _us_, and sent us so he can redeem others through us
- Jesus' death brings peace with the Father, plus a basis for how we interact with the world
- **Ex. Brian Warth** (pastor, Chapel of Change, south LA; autobiography _Young Man Arise_)
    - Grew up in LA gang culture; brother killed at 15; joined a gang at 12; convicted of murder at 16, life sentence
    - Heard the gospel in prison, was discipled by chaplains, taught Bible studies; released after 16 years
    - Planted Chapel of Change in a diverse, "deeply broken" area
    - His story of redemption shapes his preaching (hope, freedom from guilt and shame)
- Only those who've experienced reconciliation will be effective agents of it
- Peace with God lets us trust his sovereignty while working earnestly
#### 3. Sent to a foreign land
- Not unique to our age; every generation (Paul, Augustine, Billy Graham, "you")
- Mission doesn't change, but the culture does, so study it like ambassadors
- Behind outrage is real need
    - Anger and polarization are self-defense mechanisms over real questions of origin, identity, purpose, path
    - People are busier and more connected than ever, but that covers a crisis of identity, purpose, and belonging
##### Crisis #1: Lost identity
- Identity has become a primary idol (ch. 5)
- Pascal: the empty "print and trace" of happiness, which only an infinite object (God) can fill
- Culture asks "Who am I?" and "Where do I belong?"; identity politics and polarization follow from different answers
- Jesus gives an identity above temporal political and cultural ones
- Evangelism can't stay online; need real participation in people's lives
##### Crisis #2: Misplaced purpose
- _Time_ (2015): attention span of 8 seconds vs a goldfish's 9 (Microsoft study)
    - 77% of 18-24 year-olds reach for their phone when nothing occupies them
    - Lots of data, no communal context to process it
- Humans are hardwired to work (Adam and Eve in the Garden); no purpose leads to drift, boredom, compulsive anxiety
- Schopenhauer: boredom as the "empty longing for a new desire"
- Boredom leads to outrage and harm; **outrage becomes a misplaced substitute purpose**
- Gospel gives a new purpose: reconciliation, a new calling to go back into a now-foreign world
##### Crisis #3: Chronic loneliness
- UK created a cabinet position on loneliness (Jan 2018); 9M+ adults affected
- More connected than ever, yet loneliness is an epidemic
- Cacioppo: loneliness is human; it's serious when it becomes a self-reinforcing loop
- Much anger is a response to loneliness, so ambassadors should feel compassion
- David Brooks: secular people must build their own communities; religions come with covenantal rituals
- **Ex. CrossFit** mimics church
    - "Temples" (gyms), authority structure (coaches), guiding documents (programs), "evangelizing" testimonies
    - Nothing wrong with it, but it's a substitute and shadow of the church
    - What happens when someone "can't make the cut" or breaks a leg?
- Church can offer authentic relationship, restoration, renewal; Jesus gives adoption into a family and a home
### The Outrage of Distracted Ambassadors
- Much outrage at Christians comes from taking our eyes off the ambassador identity
    - Advancing any mission other than Christ's forfeits the gospel's healing power and breeds division
- **Mission creep** (military term; Taw and Peters)
    - Objectives expand beyond the original goals, diluting purpose (Beirn and Murray)
    - Ex. Stetzer at the Billy Graham Center: asked of every program, "How does this advance the mission?"
- Christians' image has taken a beating largely because of adding political, cultural, or social goals to the gospel
- 2 Corinthians 4:5-7: "we don't preach about ourselves," we're fragile clay jars, and the power is God's
- Many Christians engage in outrage rather than offering the antidote
    - Competing missions and "alternative kingdoms" lead to conflict and a damaged witness
- Self-assessment: if the community knows us as anything other than followers of Christ, a peripheral mission has crept in
    - Neighbours shouldn't be surprised by our values and beliefs
### Compassionately Love the People
- Fundamental to the role: abiding love for those we're sent to
- **Matthew 9:35-38**: Jesus had compassion on the crowds, "harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd"
    - Greek word for _compassion_ means to be moved in the bowels, a "gut reaction"
    - Ex. Stetzer's feeling as a father of three daughters
    - We're not dispassionate ambassadors; we're called to love the foreign land
- Having been reconciled, we've received the same Spirit and should have compassion for those still restless
#### Compassion through prayer
- Matthew 9:37-38 progression
    1. The foreign land is broken, desolate, and in danger
    2. Not enough ambassadors
    3. Pray for God to send more
- **LifeWay Research (the head/heart gap)**
    - 80% agree they have a personal responsibility to share their faith
    - 75% feel comfortable doing so
    - But 61% hadn't told anyone how to become a Christian in six months
    - 48% hadn't invited anyone to church in six months
- Cause: ignoring Jesus' call to pray makes us apathetic; remembering we were once lost sparks compassion
- Jonathan Edwards: prayer divides believers from "posers" (he said hypocrites)
    - Neglecting prayer is inconsistent with supreme love for God
- "The worker is you"; praying for the harvest includes praying for our own hearts as sent ones
- **LifeWay prayer study** (with Max Lucado, _Before Amen_), among Americans who pray
    - 41% have prayed for people who mistreat them; 57% for enemies
    - 21% prayed to win the lottery; 13% for a favourite team; 5% for someone to get fired
    - Most pray for family, friends, own problems, own sin (42%), disasters (38%), thanksgiving (37%)
    - Only 20% regularly pray for people of other faiths or no faith
    - More people have prayed to win the lottery than regularly pray for the lost
#### Compassion through action
- Danger of theology without application, like skipping leg day
- Can't leave all the work to pastors and leaders
- **Ex. neighbour map**: Stetzer mapped his eight nearest neighbours who didn't know the Lord, built relationships, led one family to Christ and baptized the parents _(details on p. 274, not included)_
- "Here I am, Lord, send me" (Isaiah 6:8): it begins with one step of obedience
- Too often our solutions look like the world's
    - Grace for people who look and struggle like us, scorn for those who don't
    - Ex. people raising uncomfortable topics like racial injustice get dismissed, which is profoundly unloving
    - Church becomes another platform for the world's outrage, defined by politics instead of being an outpost of the coming Kingdom
    - Silenced people seek other communities, which can offer love but not the healing of the gospel
- **Brian Warth story**: asked to officiate the funeral of a girl killed in a street shooting (her mom was in gangs)
    - The mother later gave her life to Jesus and now leads Celebrate Recovery at Chapel of Change
    - Brian: his story helps him see people "with the eyes of a doctor rather than a judge"
    - Ties to Mark 2:17 (Jesus came for the sick, not the well)

#### Conclusion: the mirror and the door

- Can't sit in comfortable churches waiting out the cultural storm; study the crises, find the pain, respond with gospel truth and compassion
- "What are we known for?" leads to
    - A **mirror**: who we are (Christ-followers far from home, students and intercessors for the community)
    - A **door**: a foreign land hostile to the message but longing for what Jesus offers
- Go armed with the gospel, prayer (God's Spirit gives compassion), and the church (gives community)
- Churches need to move from destructive infighting and mission creep to God's mission
- Change is possible: the Nashville church went from fighting to "ambassadors to the outraged for Jesus"
