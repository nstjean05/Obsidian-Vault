- Three views of sex and gender:
	1. Sex and gender are exclusively biological
	2. Sex is biological and gender is closely linked to sex; but gender can go beyond biology
	- Each of these may be viewed as *gender essentialism*
		- Fixed traits, sex/gender are male/female, generative thus functionally opposing, crucial for human flourishing, sacramental

- Important terminology
	- Sex
		- Biological
		- Male/Female, XX/XY
		- Gamete size (sperm/egg)
	- Sex Characteristics
		- Observable traits associated with being male or female
		- Resultant from biological factors
		- Includes primary and secondary characteristics
	- Gender
		- Psychological, behavioural, social, cultural characteristics associated with being male and female. (APA, 2019)
	- Gender Identity
		- Psychological sense of self in relation to sex.
		- "Deeply felt, inherent sense of being a boy, a man, or male; a girl, a woman, or female..." (APA, 2019)
	- Gender Dysphoria
		- Discomfort or distress related to perceived incongruence between one's gender identity and sex.
	- Intersex
		- Umbrella term to describe variation in make and female sex characteristics. Intersex people can have different, or a mixture of, sex characteristics (e.g. sex chromosomes, reproductive organs, etc.) (adapted from intersexcanada.org)
- Sexual Differentiation
	- Hormones - Chemicals, secreted by endocrine glands, that regulate target organs or tissues via the bloodstream
	- Organization effects - Permanent alterations of the nervous system and subsequent behaviour
	- Activation effects - Transient change in the behaviour of an adult animal due to influence of hormones
	- Sex Determination
		- Process by which a fetus will develop as male or female
		- Starts at the chromosome level
			- Mother - X
			- Father - X or Y
		- Indifferent gonads develop into testes or ovaries during the first month of differentiation
	- Gonads
		- SRY gene (sex determining region of the Y)
		- Encodes protein that directs internal (core) cells of the indifferent gonad to proliferate
		- Result = testis
		- No Y-Chromosome = No SRY gene
		- By default, external cells of the gonad differentiate
		- Result = ovaries
	- Masculinization - Wolffian system - Testosterone ((de)masculinization)
	- Defeminization - Mullerian system - AMH ((de) feminization)
	- Other hormones (ex. DHT) catalyze the formation of physical sex differences
- Differences of Sexual Development
	- Turner's syndrome (X0)
	- Androgen intensivity (AIS)
		- Often these women identify as such
		- Genetically male
	- Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia
		- A masculinization of external genitilia
		- Most live as hetero women, but are above the average for not being so
	- Guevodoces
		- Can't convert in DHT (they lack 5 alpha reductase)
		- Raised as girls, but usually switch immediately to boys at puberty
		- Don't know this until puberty
		- A genetic condition which can be passed on

	- How should we classify sex?
		- Non-Binary - Binary system is too simplistic, should be a spectrum (Nature, 2015)
		- However, intersex conditions cannot be easily classified
		- DSDs introduce variability within sexes
		- Binary - Humans are anisogamous; there are only two gametes; no available evidence for variance in either
		- Intersex conditions are rare, and are variations within male/female
- What, then, of gender?
	- There isn't a particularly reliable way of determining whether a given brain is male or female
	- Looking at statistical averages, some brain areas are often larger/smaller in the male or female brains compared to one another
		- Influenced hormonally
	- Male and female sex differences follow a very similar bell curve, offset by a few points
	- Common differences in empathy, spatial abilities, etc.
		- A ton of overlap - any one trait or small collection will not determine male/female
