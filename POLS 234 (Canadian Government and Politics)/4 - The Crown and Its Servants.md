## 4.1 Constitutional Monarchy
- Canada is a **constitutional monarchy**
	- **Monarchical by law, democratic by convention**
	- All governmental power formally vested in the Crown
	- In practice, power exercised democratically through responsible government
- **King Charles III of Britain** is also **King Charles III of Canada**
	- Separate legal identity for each realm
	- Cannot be in Canada most of the time
	- Represented by **Governor General** in Canada

## 4.2 The Crown and Governor General
- **Governor General:** Representative of the Crown in Canada
	- Recommended by PM to the Crown for 5-year term
	- Acts as **Head of State**
	- Functions as **steward/backstop of responsible government**
- **Key functions of Governor General:**
	1. Appoints the Prime Minister
	2. Ensures democratic linkage between government and House of Commons is maintained
	3. Acts on advice of ministers (convention)
	4. Can refuse PM's request to call election if viable alternative government exists in House
- **Reserve Powers:** Rarely used powers to intervene in political deadlock
	- Ex. 1975 Australian Governor General dismissed PM during deadlock (very controversial)
	- Mere threat of dismissal usually enough for PM to step down

## 4.3 The Cabinet
- **Cabinet:** Composed of MPs with confidence of House majority
	- Although has no constitutional power of its own, achieves **fusion of powers** through control of executive and legislative branches
- **Practically speaking:** Cabinet exercises executive power (not the Crown)
- **Queen's Privy Council for Canada:** Formal advisory body to Crown
	- All current and former cabinet ministers
	- Only current cabinet ministers active
- **Collective Responsibility:** Convention requiring cabinet to function as team
	- All ministers must publicly support cabinet decisions
	- Must present united front even if privately disagreed
	- Ministers who cannot support decision must resign

## 4.4 Cabinet Structure
- **Ministers of State:** Junior ministers assisting senior ministers
- **Parliamentary Secretaries:** MPs who assist ministers but not in cabinet
- **Treasury Board:** Special cabinet committee established by statute
	- Reviews all proposed expenditures
	- Serves as official employer of civil service

## 4.6 The Prime Minister
- PM is most powerful political figure, but powers are **conventional, not constitutional**
	- Constitution Act 1867 barely mentions PM
	- Powers arise from responsible government conventions
- **PM is NOT Head of State** (that's the Crown/Governor General)
- **Key PM powers:**
	1. Advise Crown on cabinet appointments (appoint/dismiss ministers at will)
	2. Chair cabinet meetings (determines consensus, no formal voting)
	3. Leading spokesperson for government
	4. Advise Governor General on dissolution of Parliament/elections
	5. Advise on key appointments (Senate, judiciary)
	6. Organize government machinery (departments, cabinet committees)

### PM Support Staff
- **Prime Minister's Office (PMO)**
	- 100+ partisan political activists
	- Provides political advice
	- Led by Chief of Staff
- **Privy Council Office (PCO)**
	- Non-partisan career civil servants
	- Provides policy advice
	- Headed by Chief Clerk (highest-ranking civil servant)
- **Deputy Prime Minister**
	- Cabinet member serving as second-in-command
	- Role varies by PM (some use extensively, Harper/early Trudeau had none)

## 4.7 Prime Ministerial Government?
- **Debate:** Moving from cabinet government → "prime ministerial government"
- **Evidence for stronger PM:**
	- Digital age gives PM massive public profile vs. cabinet
	- Jean Chrétien: Cabinet is PM's "focus group"
	- Harper example: Quebec nation resolution without consulting relevant minister (Michael Chong)
- **But PM power has limits:**
	- Not directly elected
	- Dependent on House confidence
	- Cannot alienate caucus or cabinet
	- Popular PMs command support; unpopular PMs struggle
	- Provincial examples: Campbell (BC), Stelmach/Redford (AB), Selinger (MB) forced out

## 4.8 The Civil Service
- ~275,000 federal civil servants (2019)
- **Non-partisan professionals** hired on merit by Public Service Commission
- **Two branches:**
	1. **Line departments:** Provide services (Transport, Health, Foreign Affairs)
	2. **Central agencies:** Coordinate policy (PCO, Finance, Treasury Board)
- **Ministerial Responsibility:** Minister accountable to House for every civil servant in department
	- Must investigate allegations of incompetence/impropriety
	- Resign if substantial mismanagement by minister personally
	- **Not personally accountable for others' mistakes** (Wilson budget leak 1988)
	- **Politically accountable:** Must ensure corrective measures
- **Deputy Minister:** Top career civil servant in each department
	- Often knows more than minister about department
	- Theory: Chief advisor
	- Practice: May really run department (especially with weak minister)

## Key Terms
- **Constitutional Monarchy:** Monarchical by law, democratic by convention
- **Collective Responsibility:** Cabinet functions as team on public policy
- **Ministerial Responsibility:** Minister accountable for civil servants in their department
- **Caucus:** Team of MPs from a party
- **Reserve Powers:** Governor General's rarely-used intervention powers
- **Fusion of Powers:** Cabinet controls both executive and legislative branches
