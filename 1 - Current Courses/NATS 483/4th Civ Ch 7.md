3 - Suppose you are in charge of an agency responsible for a medical insurance scheme run by the state. Which (if any) of the following treatments should the plan pay for, or not pay for, and why? 

Open-heart surgery on a 90- year-old, when the average life span is 80. Would it make a difference if the average life span were 150? An experimental procedure (40 percent chance of success) to remove a tumor from the brain of an infant who has otherwise an 80 percent chance of dying within a year. The surgery in b when without it the child would have impaired hearing and sight but otherwise live normally. The surgery in b performed in another country at a cost higher than maintaining a bed in a hospital in the home country for an entire year. A sex-change operation for a patient who claims to be in great distress over being in the wrong kind of body. A sex-change operation for a patient who has already transitioned from male to female, now has second thoughts and wants to transition back.

Gender-affirming surgery for an infant born with a mix of female and male organs (intersex). (Note that although such surgery has in the past been routinely recommended in such rare cases, it can cause permanent scarring and lasting pain when done on infants, but this outcome is less likely when done for adults.) Sterilization (tubal ligations and vasectomies) for those who wish to have no further children but have no organic malfunction. Take into consideration that there are non-invasive means of achieving the latter. Tubal or ovarian repairs (30 percent success rate) for a woman who wants to bear a first child. Surgery in the previous case for a woman who already had two children; five children; ten children. An in vitro fertilization (“test-tube baby”) for a couple who are infertile. A new and experimental drug whose preliminary tests indicate may relieve some symptoms and postpone death from a new and rare type of infection by as much as a year but whose

and side effects are unknown. A year’s supply is estimated to cost $30,000 per person. m. A $30 000 pacemaker for an 85-year-old woman. Radiation treatments and/or costly surgery for an 80-year-old man with prostate cancer. Leg lengthening surgery for a teenager who wants to be taller so she can make the high school basketball team. Plastic surgery to remove small and harmless but unpleasant-looking growths from the face of a teenage boy. Surgery for a worker in zero gravity who wants her legs replaced with arms and hands because the former are of no use there, and the latter would make her more productive. A liver transplant for a boy whose body has already rejected two livers and whose older brother died after unsuccessful surgery of the same type. A transplant of the brain of an elderly man, the rest of whose body has experienced multiple

failures so that he is on life support, into the head of a woman whose brain was effectively destroyed in an automobile accident, though the rest of her body is functional (the plot of an old science fiction novel). The implant of a computing device in the head of a person with (i) damage in the portion of her brain responsible for computation; (ii) an accountant who wants to be able to quickly add columns of figures from ancient paper documents without having to type them into a spreadsheet; (iii) a day trader who wants to be able to make faster decisions on buying and selling stocks to maximize her daily profits; (iv) someone who wants extra memory storage capacity for their profession. Cosmetic surgery to make the patient look younger and prolong her career as an actress, or entertainer. Surgery to shorten a man’s left hand and foot to match the smaller right ones. Would it make a difference to your answer if functionality and appearance were not an issue, and the only reason the patient cites is the money to be saved by

having to purchase two pairs of gloves and shoes so as to fit both feet and both hands? Surgery to create gill-like structures in the lungs of a diver so she can effectively breathe the water and extract oxygen from it as a fish does. The use of a drug that in tests appears to enhance intelligence. The use of a very expensive drug that is known to be capable of temporarily slowing a deadly cancer, but that at best extends patient life by three to six months. What if the drug has life-threatening side effects of its own in up to five percent of patients? ten percent? twenty percent? Tests or treatments done in a neighboring country at considerable expense, but in a timely manner that can save lives. The country of residence has a medical system that is underfunded and overwhelmed with patients, so cannot do all the requisite tests or treatments in a timely way.
