2 - If a machine passes Turing’s test, claiming for itself self-awareness, does that mean that it is self-aware? How can one tell? Or, does it matter if one cannot?
	Passing the Turing test is neither necessary nor sufficient for self awareness. One can image something that is aware of itself, but could not be able to convince you of its humanity. For instance, my dog seems somewhat self-aware, knowing whether she has done something bad. However, my little Dachshund could never convince me that she is human. On the other end of the spectrum, many modern AI models pass the Turing test, and with robotics in the future will also become Turing-complete. However, that does not mean they are truly self-aware - they simply are excellent at replicating it.
	It is challenging to say whether it matters if an AI is truly self-aware, since we don't totally understand the nature of awareness and consciousness. The only person you can tell is conscious is oneself, and even then we are not totally sure where it comes from. From circumstantial evidence you can infer that others are conscious, since they exhibit the same behaviours and sensibilities as you do, but there isn't an absolute way to prove it. In some sense, it doesn't matter if a humanoid robot is actually conscious, as once it becomes indistinguishable from a human, then it will likely be treated as such. But perhaps, if consciousness arises from complexity, a suitably advanced AI can generate it.

8 - Discuss the technical problems involved in simulating vision in a machine.
	There are many technical problems in simulating vision. One of these is the density of compute required, as the brain is exceptionally efficient at processing visuals. A computer is very fast, but also quite bulky, especially for cutting-edge technology. Often this leads to much of it being done remotely, leading to some latency.
	Other challenges arise with the identification of visuals. As AI has developed in the last decade, massive strides have been made in computer vision techniques. However, they are still imperfect. CAPTCHAs still stop many bots, but fewer and fewer, since they've been used as training material. Computer's also aren't good at always noticing where in the frame of view to direct their focus - something that the human brain does naturally.
	Finally, there are technical challenges in the sensors used to take in light. Modern cameras have gotten very good, but are still poor in many circumstances. The autonomous EV manufacturer Tesla decided to use cameras instead of LiDAR, since this better replicates how a human would view the road. But these cameras are imperfect at times, such as extremely rainy weather, or when the sun is on the horizon directly facing them. This highlights how strong human vision is, but highlights one of its greatest weaknesses - changing it in the traditional method takes a very long time. Humans can do fine in these edge cases, but by utilizing technologies like LiDAR, IR, and ultrasonic, machines have a much higher potential capability.

10 - Write a paper attacking the concept of human brain downloading on either scientific or moral grounds, or both.
	There are many reasons why it is unethical and unhelpful to the human race to conduct brain downloads. Pragmatically, this would be a disaster. Currently, the people pursuing transhumanism the most are in the wealthiest 0.01% of society. The Alcor Life Extension Foundation, the largest cryopreservation vault, currently hosts 5 of the world's 50 wealthiest individuals at $200,000 for full body preservation. Anyone in lower income brackets would likely not be able to afford this care, exacerbating the already significant wealth imbalance. The wealthy today would gain access to the technology, and then be able to pull the ladder up after them. Corporate consolidation would continue, and it is easy to see how this would lead to a dystopian future. Peter Thiel of Palantir and other wealthy AI CEOs have already been promoting the concept of a *permanent underclass*, which they believe will consist of everyone who is not an owner of the AI company to achieve AGI. While this should be a fantastical trajectory for society, it is being realistically discussed.
	Technologically, there are also many challenges with brain downloading. The brain is exceptionally complex, and at our current point we do not have a good understanding of all its mechanics. This would likely lead to a simplification of it during the digitization process, and create questions about whether the resulting consciousness was really human.
