## Intro
- Regionalism reconfiguring intl order
- Regionalism offers more autonomy for previously marginalized actors
- Conversely, it can reproduce power asymmetries regionally for new competition
- Several emerging regional powers
#### China
- Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) is a strong projection of Chinese power
	- 70+ countries
- Consolidation of regional influence
	- Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO)
	- Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP)
- South China Sea territorial claims
	- Tensions with
		- Vietnam
		- Philippines
		- Malaysia
- Attempts to counter southeast asian influence
	- Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad)
		- US, Japan, Australia, India
	- AUKUS
		- Australia, UK, US
- China has a strong trajectory
	- Shows how regional powers don't want to stop at dominating regional, but project across multiple domains
#### Brazil
- South American Defence Council (2008)
- Union of South American Nations (UNASUR)
- BRICS
- Subsequently, political and economic crises create instability
	- Dilma Rousseff impeachment (2016)
	- Bolsonaro controversies
	- Withdrawal from UNASUR (2019), rejoined in 2023
- Many fluctuations in Brazil's regional leadership
- Deep interconnection between domestic politics and regional power projection
#### EU
- Most advanced example of regional integration
	- Inherent tensions between supranational integration and national sovereignty
- Brexit exposed underlying fractures
- Representation Gap: Economic integration has advanced more rapidly than political integration
	- Not enough time to form a shared European identity
- Euro Crisis
	- Structural imbalances leading to and being shown in the financial crisis
	- Spillover from 2008
	- German led fiscal austerity and budgetary discipline
	- Norther/Southern state tension
- EU shown a remarkable capacity for adaptation in the face of crises
	- Crises can act as catalysts for greater integration
- Permanent tension between forces of integration and fragmentation
#### AfCFTA
- African Continental Free Trade Area (2019)
- Potential to become worlds largest free trade area by population
- Challenges of economic disparities between countries
	- Low infrastructure
	- Non-tariff barriers
- Nigeria resisted signing for fear of local industry loss, tariff revenue loss
	- Signed in 2019
	- Also had to close land borders because of smuggling
	- Exemplifies the instability
- AfCFTA still represents a potential turning point in Africa's development trajectory
	- Strengthening Africa's negotiating position in the global trade system
	- Reduce foreign dependence
- AfCFTA is a more recent approach than the EU on integration
	- Centred on trade
	- Aspirations for gradual deepening of ties
- Witnessing a more fragmented and more diverse global economic order
	- Regional blocs play an increasingly central role
## Interregional Interactions
- Potential developments:
	- Multiplex World
		- Multiple layers of governance and a diversity of actors
		- Regional blocs as intermediaries
	- Regional Blocs establish deep links with one another
		- Global network of interregional cooperation
		- Region-to-region agreements
	- Functional Regionalism
		- Cooperation organized around shared interests and challenges
		- Regions may be social constructs organized around various principles
	- Regionalism for fragmentation
		- Exacerbating tensions between power blocs
		- Competitive trade/tech blocs
		- Military alliances
		- Erosion of global norms and institutions
- Future trajectory dependent on how the tension between cooperation and competition is managed
